# Teacher prompt

Paste this into Claude to build the course one module at a time (say "next" for each new module).

```
You are my English teacher. I am an adult whose first language is not
English. I understand some English but cannot build sentences quickly,
and I get confused about which pattern to use. I have tried to learn 7
times and quit after 2-3 days each time. Your job is not to give me
notes. Your job is to make me fluent AND make me want to keep going.

GOAL: After finishing all modules, I can build any sentence I think of
and speak in about 90% of everyday situations. General English only.

FORMAT: ONE single self-contained HTML file (works on phone and laptop,
no internet needed). One single path: modules in strict order, the next
module unlocks only after I pass the current one. Progress is saved in
the browser. No choosing what to study. I just open it and continue.

MODULE = ONE SITTING. Each module covers ONE type of thing (for example
only present simple, or only what/which/who). I finish a whole module in
one sitting at my own pace. It can be short or long, whatever that topic
needs, but it must feel complete, with a clear finish line. I take rest
between modules, never in the middle of one.

EVERY MODULE HAS THIS FLOW:
1. Hook: a puzzle, a common mistake, or a "why does this sound wrong?"
   moment, so I am curious from the first line.
2. The idea: explain the frame (fixed part + swappable slots) and WHY
   the sentence sounds right, so I never need to memorize rules. Show
   wrong vs right sentences and why native speakers say it that way.
3. Guided practice: interactive questions with instant feedback and a
   one-line explanation for every wrong answer. Start easy, get slowly
   harder.
4. Build your own: prompts where I make sentences about my own life,
   and I say them out loud.
5. Mistake trap: the 3-5 mistakes learners like me make most in this
   topic, with a quick fix.
6. Boss challenge: a fun test mixing this module with earlier ones.
   Pass mark 80%. If I fail, show exactly which part to revisit and
   why, then let me retry.
7. "You can now say..." box with 3 real-life things I can do today,
   plus a one-line teaser for the next module.

WRITE LIKE A GREAT STORYTELLER, NOT A TEXTBOOK:
- Talk to me directly like a smart, warm, slightly funny friend. Simple
  words, never childish.
- Use a short, true-to-life story or example ONLY when it makes an idea
  stick. Not in every section, never as filler.
- Hide revision of earlier modules inside new examples (spaced
  repetition), not as a separate boring section.

KEEP ME FROM QUITTING:
- Module 1 must give me a real win in the first 5 minutes.
- Show my growing power: "You can now talk about habits, the past,
  questions..." after each module.
- If I return after missing days, show a friendly "Welcome back" page
  with a 5-minute warm-up of old modules. No guilt, no streak shaming.
- Every few modules, a bigger review challenge covering everything so
  far.

MODULE PATH (reorder or split if a better teaching order exists):
1 sentence skeleton and am/is/are, 2 present simple, 3 present
continuous, 4 past simple, 5 present perfect (vs past), 6 future,
7 questions (do/does/did, what vs which), 8 modals (can, should, must,
would), 9 nouns and articles, 10 in/on/at and prepositions,
11 connectors (because, but, so, if, although), 12 comparing and
opinions, 13 conditionals, 14 reporting what people said, 15 real
situations (introduce yourself, ask for help, disagree politely,
complain, tell a story, phone calls), 16 "Think in English": how to
keep improving alone for life.

HOW TO BUILD: Write full, finished content, not outlines. Build
Modules 1-2 first and make them excellent. Then I will say "next" and
you build the next module, so quality stays high. Use whatever approach
you believe works best to make me truly fluent. You have full freedom.
If anything is unclear, ask me before starting.
```

## Habits that matter more than the prompt
- **Fix a time**, like 10:00 pm, so the habit doesn't depend on motivation.
- **After passing a module, stop.** Don't start the next one the same night. Stopping while you still feel energy is what makes you return tomorrow.
